Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 October 2026

ClipQ (“the app”) is an Android video editor made by Saral Labs, published as com.dip.clipq. This policy explains what the app does with your data.

The short version

Your photos, videos, music and voice recordings never leave your phone. ClipQ has no account, no server of its own and no ads. It counts which screens and tools are used, through Google Analytics, so we know what to improve first.

What ClipQ reads

  • Your gallery, if you allow it. When you start a video, add a clip, make an AutoCut or extract audio, ClipQ shows your videos and photos inside the app so the newest clip is at the top. Android asks first: you can allow all of them, allow only the ones you select, or decline and pick with the Android photo picker instead. ClipQ reads the list and the thumbnails only to show them to you on screen. It opens only the items you add to a project, and never copies, uploads or analyses the rest.
  • Photos for collages, posters and photo looks come through the Android photo picker, so ClipQ only sees the photos you choose there.
  • Audio files you pick. Songs you add are read from where they are. ClipQ does not copy them unless you ask it to extract audio from a video.
  • Your microphone, only while you record a voiceover. Recording starts when you press record and stops when you press stop. The recording is saved in ClipQ’s private storage and becomes part of your project. It is never uploaded.
  • Your camera and microphone, only while the teleprompter is open. The camera shows a preview so you can frame yourself, and records only between your taps on record and stop. Each take is saved in ClipQ’s private storage. It is never uploaded, and nothing the camera sees is analysed.
  • The teleprompter script you type. It is kept in ClipQ’s settings on your phone so it is still there next time.
  • .cube colour files and .clipq draft files you open.

What ClipQ stores, and where

Everything is stored in ClipQ’s private app storage on your phone:

  • Your projects (drafts): the list of clips, trims, text and settings. These hold links to your media, not copies of it.
  • Voiceovers you record, teleprompter takes, audio you extract from videos, and sound effects you add. Takes you discard are deleted straight away.
  • Reversed copies of clips you reverse, and colour files you import.
  • Your settings, such as theme and export quality.

Finished videos are saved to your gallery in the Movies/ClipQ album. They are yours to keep, share or delete like any other video.

You can see and clear everything ClipQ stores from the Storage tab. Uninstalling ClipQ removes all of it except the videos you saved to your gallery.

What ClipQ sends over the internet

Nothing from your media. All editing, beat detection, filters, voice effects and exporting happen on your phone, and work with no connection. ClipQ shows no ads.

Usage analytics. Which screens and tools are used, described in the next section.

Diagnostics from Google’s on-phone components. Google’s ML Kit, which finds the person for background removal and the cutout collage, reports metrics about itself to Google, such as how long it took and whether it worked, as described in ML Kit’s data disclosure. It never sends your photos or videos.

In-app updates and reviews. Google Play checks whether a newer version of ClipQ is available, and may show Play’s rating sheet. Google handles this under Google’s privacy policy.

Optional caption download. When you choose to download auto captions, Google Play downloads them to your phone. After that, captions are made on your phone: your audio is never sent anywhere. Google Play services may likewise download the on-phone model that finds objects in a photo for background removal.

ClipQ has no account, no sign-in, and no crash reporting service of its own.

Usage analytics

ClipQ uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count which screens are opened and which tools are used — for example “the Blend panel was opened”, “a 1080p video was saved” or “a sticker was added” — so we know which features to improve first. With each event Google also records the app version, the phone model, the Android version, the language, an approximate country derived from the IP address, and a random identifier for this installation of the app.

Analytics never receives a photo, a video, a voice recording, a song, a caption, any text you type, or a file or project name. Collection of the advertising identifier and ad personalisation are both switched off. Google processes these events on our behalf as described in Firebase privacy and security and keeps them for a limited time. Clearing ClipQ’s data in Android settings resets the installation identifier.

Backups

ClipQ opts out of Android cloud backup for its projects and files, because drafts point to media that may not exist on another phone. To move a project to another phone, use Save draft file to create a .clipq file, which you control.

Permissions

  • Photos and videos (read only) let ClipQ show your gallery when you add clips, as described above. ClipQ asks the first time you open its gallery, and if you decline, or allow only some items, the Android photo picker still works for everything. On Android 12 and older this is the storage read permission.
  • Internet sends the usage analytics and Google component diagnostics described above. Editing and saving never need it.
  • Microphone records voiceovers and the sound of teleprompter takes, only while you are recording. ClipQ asks the first time you tap record or open the teleprompter, and every other feature works without it.
  • Camera is used only by the teleprompter, to preview and record you. ClipQ asks the first time you open the teleprompter, and every other feature works without it.
  • Notifications show export progress so you can leave the app while a video saves.
  • Foreground service keeps an export running when the screen is off.
  • Vibrate gives a small tick when a trim snaps. You can switch it off in Settings.
  • Storage write (Android 9 and older only) is needed to save finished videos to the gallery on those versions.

Children

ClipQ is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.
