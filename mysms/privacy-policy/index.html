Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

My SMS is a messaging app by Saral Labs, published on Google Play as com.dip.mysms. It replaces your phone’s SMS app and sorts your messages into OTPs, transactions, bills, offers and personal chats. The short version: your messages, and everything My SMS works out from them, stay on your phone.

The short version

  • Your messages, and everything My SMS works out from them, stay on your phone.
  • My SMS has no account, no sign-in and no server. Nothing about your messages or money is uploaded.
  • My SMS has no ads and does not hold Android’s internet permission. Messages you send go through your carrier, and app updates and review prompts come through the Google Play Store app.

What My SMS reads, and why

SMS and MMS. As your default SMS app, My SMS must read, store, send and receive your messages. They stay in Android’s own message store on your phone, where they were before. My SMS reads them to show your conversations, find one-time passwords, and pick out bank transactions, bills and coupon codes.

Spending, bills and coupons. My SMS reads bank and card alerts on your phone to work out your monthly spending by category and by card. It also finds bill due dates for reminders and keeps coupon codes in a wallet. The results are saved in a private database inside the app on your phone. They are not sent anywhere.

Contacts. Used only to show names instead of numbers and to suggest recipients. Contacts are read on the phone and never copied out of it.

Phone state. Used only to list your SIM cards, so that on a dual-SIM phone you can choose which one sends.

Notifications. Used to tell you about new messages and upcoming bills.

What leaves your phone

  • Messages you send go through your mobile carrier, as with any SMS app.
  • Picture messages are downloaded and sent by Android’s own MMS service over your carrier’s network.
  • Play services. The Google Play Store app checks for and installs updates to My SMS, and may show a review prompt. Google handles that under its own privacy policy.
  • Things you share. When you share a split summary or a message, it goes to the app you pick.

What My SMS does not do

  • It does not upload, back up or sync your messages or financial data.
  • It does not sell or share your data with anyone.
  • It does not show ads or contain an advertising SDK.
  • It does not include analytics or crash reporting SDKs.
  • It excludes its database from Android cloud backup and device transfer.

Permissions

PermissionWhy
READ_SMS, RECEIVE_SMS, SEND_SMSTo show, receive and send your text messages as the default SMS app.
RECEIVE_MMS, RECEIVE_WAP_PUSHTo receive picture and group messages. Android requires these of every default SMS app.
READ_CONTACTSTo show names instead of numbers and to suggest recipients. Optional.
READ_PHONE_STATETo list your SIM cards so you can choose which one sends.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS, VIBRATEFor new-message alerts and bill reminders.
RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETEDSo bill reminders survive a restart of the phone.
WAKE_LOCK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATENot declared by us. Added by Android’s background-work library, which runs the bill reminders and the first scan of your bank messages. It only reads whether a network is present; My SMS has no internet permission.

My SMS does not support RCS chat. Only Google Messages can access RCS on Android.

Splitting expenses

Groups, people and expenses you add to Split stay on your phone. When you share a summary, you choose the app it goes to. My SMS doesn’t send it anywhere itself.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling My SMS deletes its database. Your messages stay in Android’s message store, where your next SMS app will find them. You can delete messages, transactions, bills and coupons in the app at any time. You can also turn on automatic deletion of OTP messages after a day.

Children

My SMS is not directed at children under 13.
